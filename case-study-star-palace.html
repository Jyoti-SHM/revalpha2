Case study

Star Palace, Rameshwaram.

A pilgrim and leisure hotel in one of India’s most seasonal markets. Here is how we approached it.

~15%

The situation

Star Palace operates in Rameshwaram, a market driven by pilgrimage travel and seasonal leisure demand. Demand is intense around festivals and pilgrimage periods, then falls away sharply. Rates were being set broadly, and the property’s online presence did not reflect the quality of the guest experience.

What we did

We treated the calendar as the primary asset. Rates were aligned to pilgrimage periods, festivals and local demand patterns rather than a single seasonal rate. Availability and rate plans were rebuilt across the OTAs so the property competed properly for the dates that mattered. Reputation was managed alongside pricing, because in a market where guests choose on reviews, ranking and rate are the same conversation.

The outcome

Over the engagement, the property grew revenue by approximately 15% year on year and reached the number one position on TripAdvisor in its market. The gains came from pricing discipline, better distribution and a stronger reputation, not from cutting rates.

What this shows

Seasonal properties do not need more demand. They need pricing that moves with the demand they already have, and distribution that makes the most of it.

Results are specific to this property and its market. Individual results vary with demand, product quality, reputation, operations and market conditions. RevAlpha does not guarantee revenue outcomes.
